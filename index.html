08 / STATEMENT OF PURPOSE

A research vision
for the ATLAS PhD.

My research interests focus on advancing Physics Education Research (PER) through computational creativity and emerging educational technologies. I am particularly interested in investigating how students develop deep conceptual understanding, construct and refine mental models, engage in scientific reasoning, and cultivate creativity and problem-solving abilities in physics. More specifically, I seek to explore how interactive, computational, and AI-enabled technologies can enhance students’ abilities to visualize physical phenomena, connect mathematical representations with real-world experiences, and engage meaningfully in scientific inquiry.

My research draws on the principles articulated in Professor Noah D. Finkelstein’s A Principled Way to Think About AI in Education (2025) and his collaborative work with David Perl-Nussbaum, A Framework for Institutional Change in the Age of AI (2026). These works reinforce my commitment to ensuring that technological innovation in education remains aligned with meaningful learning objectives, the advancement of human intellectual development, evidence-informed pedagogical practices, and the preservation of student agency.

Three interconnected questions motivate my proposed research:

  1. How can physics education cultivate deep conceptual understanding, scientific reasoning, critical discernment, creativity, metacognition, and intellectual independence in an era when AI increasingly performs tasks traditionally associated with human cognition?
  2. How can AI and emerging technologies transform physics teaching, learning, and assessment while strengthening, rather than diminishing, student agency, independent reasoning, educator expertise, ethical judgment, and meaningful human relationships?
  3. How must universities fundamentally rethink their educational philosophies, curricula, learning environments, and assessment practices to prepare future generations not merely to thrive in an AI-driven workforce, but also to shape a more creative, democratic, ethical, and humane society?

Addressing these questions requires an interdisciplinary research approach. Physics provides the disciplinary foundations for understanding scientific concepts and physical phenomena, while the learning sciences and cognitive science offer insights into how students construct mental models, process information, and develop scientific understanding. Computer science and engineering contribute computational methods for designing simulations, interactive environments, and intelligent learning systems. Meanwhile, design, human-computer interaction, and the humanities provide essential perspectives on creativity, human experience, accessibility, and ethical engagement with emerging technologies.

I aim to advance evidence-based, human-centered Physics Education Research by integrating scientific inquiry, computational creativity, and technological innovation to deepen conceptual understanding, foster independent scientific reasoning, and expand human intellectual potential in an increasingly AI-driven world.

I am particularly interested in collaborating with Professor Noah Finkelstein, whose research in Physics Education Research, student engagement, and educational transformation aligns closely with my interests in conceptual understanding, scientific reasoning, and human-centered physics learning. Professor Ellen Yi-Luen Do’s work in computational creativity, design cognition, tangible interaction, and creative technologies through the ACME Lab provides a valuable foundation for exploring how innovative design approaches and intelligent technologies can enrich learning. Similarly, Professor Mark D. Gross’s research in intelligent design systems, virtual environments, tangible interaction, and digital fabrication complements my interest in developing interactive, technology-enhanced physics learning environments. Their complementary expertise offers an exciting interdisciplinary foundation for advancing physics education through design, computing, and educational research.

Through interdisciplinary coursework and collaborative research, I aim to investigate how artificial intelligence (AI), interactive simulations, tangible interfaces, and mixed-reality technologies can deepen physics learning. Specifically, I am interested in designing and evaluating environments where students manipulate physical parameters, visualize abstract phenomena, formulate hypotheses, evaluate predictions, and refine conceptual understanding through experimentation. Beyond improving access to information, I seek to examine how these technologies foster independent reasoning, creativity, and critical thinking. Equally important is understanding how computational technologies can complement educators' expertise, accommodate diverse learning processes, and preserve student agency in scientific inquiry. I aspire to develop human-centered technological innovations that make physics learning more engaging, meaningful, and intellectually enriching while nurturing the curiosity and independent inquiry essential to scientific discovery.

My long-term goal is to become an interdisciplinary researcher and educator advancing Physics Education Research through computational creativity and emerging technologies. I aspire to design and evaluate innovative, evidence-based learning environments that integrate AI, interactive simulations, and mixed-reality technologies to deepen conceptual understanding, foster engagement with complex scientific phenomena, and strengthen students’ capacity for independent scientific reasoning and creative problem-solving.

The ATLAS PhD offers the interdisciplinary research environment, mentorship, and collaborative opportunities across physics, education, computer science, engineering, design, and the humanities essential to pursuing this vision. Through this program, I aim to develop the theoretical, methodological, and technological expertise needed to conceptualize, design, implement, and rigorously evaluate human-centered educational innovations.

I aspire to pursue an academic or research career, leading interdisciplinary projects that advance physics education and technology-enhanced learning. I seek to ensure that technological innovation does more than automate existing educational practices by creating new opportunities for scientific discovery, experimentation, creativity, and intellectual growth. My broader ambition is to help shape an educational future in which emerging technologies expand human intellectual potential, strengthen learner agency, and cultivate the critical thinking, creativity, and lifelong learning essential to a more innovative and humane society.

My academic background in Physics and Mathematics provided a solid foundation in mathematical reasoning, scientific inquiry, quantitative analysis, and problem-solving. These disciplines also sparked my interest in understanding how students translate mathematical abstractions into meaningful physical concepts and develop deeper scientific understanding.

Following my undergraduate studies, I worked as a Research Assistant, gaining experience in academic literature reviews, research data collection, and analytical documentation. I subsequently developed professional experience in cybersecurity, information technology, and financial systems in India and Japan, strengthening my expertise in computational problem-solving, data integrity, and the design and management of complex technological systems.

In May 2026, I completed a Master of Engineering in Engineering Management at the University of Colorado Boulder. My programming experience and completion of the Google AI Professional Certificate further expanded my understanding of computational methods and artificial intelligence, particularly their potential applications in educational research, learning technologies, and assessment.

Together, these academic and professional experiences provide a strong interdisciplinary foundation for doctoral study at ATLAS. I aim to build upon my background in physics, mathematics, engineering, and information technology while deepening my expertise in the learning sciences, computational creativity, human-computer interaction, and AI-driven educational technologies. By integrating these complementary perspectives, I hope to investigate, design, and evaluate innovative approaches that advance Physics Education Research and create more engaging, effective, and intellectually enriching learning experiences.

One research work that particularly inspires my academic aspirations is Sasha Novack, Noah Finkelstein, and Ellen Yi-Luen Do’s (2026) article, Fabrication of a Physical Circuitry Activity Booklet for English Instruction, published in Design+.

Its interdisciplinary approach illustrates how science, technology, and engineering can enrich humanities education through the design and fabrication of tangible, interactive learning experiences. I am especially interested in the researchers’ integration of design-based research, iterative prototyping, teacher collaboration, and classroom evaluation to address authentic educational needs. Their emphasis on student participation, accessibility, creativity, and contextual relevance demonstrates how thoughtful educational design can transform abstract concepts into meaningful and engaging learning experiences.

Although situated in English instruction, the study offers valuable methodological insights for Physics Education Research. I see opportunities to extend similar approaches through physical models, interactive simulations, and intelligent learning environments that enable students to visualize abstract physical phenomena, experiment with scientific principles, formulate predictions, and develop deeper conceptual understanding. Particularly compelling is the potential of tangible interaction and experiential learning to help students connect mathematical representations with physical experiences beyond conventional instructional approaches.

This work reinforces my aspiration to integrate theoretical foundations, design-based inquiry, practical experimentation, interdisciplinary collaboration, and rigorous evidence-based evaluation in my doctoral research. I hope to develop inclusive, engaging, and pedagogically meaningful innovations that transform how students experience, investigate, and understand science.
